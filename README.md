  <h1>ABSTRACT
</div>

<div align="center">
  This project presents the development and experimental evaluation of
an ultralight ion thruster intended to enhance the performance of small
fixed-wing aircraft. By systematically optimizing electrode geometry and
emitter–collector spacing, the thruster achieved a peak induced airflow
velocity of 2.9 m/s, with the best performance obtained using a 160°
electrode angle and extended spacing. A hybrid wood and 3D-printed
structural design reduced the thruster mass to 6 g, allowing integration
into a 15 g blended-wing airframe. Flight testing demonstrated
measurable improvements in both endurance (time aloft) and range
(distance travelled) compared to unpowered configurations. These
results indicate that lightweight, geometry-optimized ion propulsion can
meaningfully augment ultralight aircraft performance and represents a
scalable step toward hybrid ion–electric aviation systems.
